# SPPPD

A top-down racing game built with [LÖVE](https://love2d.org) 11.5. The handling
is built on a tyre and vehicle model, tuned for arcade fun by default, and
everything you see and hear is generated by the game: no image or sound files
are needed, though freestyle will play your own music. Sound effects are built with [sfxr.lua](https://github.com/nucular/sfxrlua)
(MIT), kept in `src/lib/`.

    make run

## Driving

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Steer | A D / arrows |
| Accelerate (at the start lights, hold the revs on the white bar) | W / up |
| Brake, then reverse | S / down |
| Handbrake (drift on Arcade) | Shift |
| Nitro on/off | Space |
| Reset to the track | R |
| Headlights (low, high) | F, G |
| Change camera | C |
| Shift gears in manual | Q, E |
| Next song in freestyle | N |
| Pause | Esc or P |

Gamepads work too, with analogue steering and triggers:

| Action | Xbox | PlayStation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steer | Left stick | Left stick |
| Accelerate, brake | RT, LT | R2, L2 |
| Handbrake | RB | R1 |
| Nitro on/off | LB | L1 |
| Shift up, down in manual | X, A | Square, Cross |
| Reset to the track | Y | Triangle |
| Change camera | Back | Share |
| Next song in freestyle | D-pad right | D-pad right |
| Pause | Start | Options |

Pads vibrate with crashes, nitro, gears, kerbs and grass, and phones buzz for
crashes and nitro. Turn VIBRATION off in Settings if you'd
rather not have it.

**Touch** controls appear on phones and tablets, and on a desktop after the first
touch. Use Settings to force them on, which lets a mouse act as one finger.

| Action | Touch |
| --- | --- |
| Steer | The stick appears under your left thumb |
| Accelerate | Automatic, or stick up with Auto Gas off (stick up revs at the start lights) |
| Brake, then reverse | Pull the stick down |
| Drift | Hold DRIFT and keep steering |
| Boost on/off | Tap BOOST |
| Reset and pause | Top right |

To try it on Android, run `make zip` and open the `.love` file in the LÖVE app, or
run `make apk` for a test build of its own. The first `make apk` compiles LÖVE for
Android, which needs the Android SDK and NDK, JDK 17 and about 1 GB in `dist/`.
Touch always uses the automatic gearbox, and the track editor isn't available on
a phone.

**Handling** decides how the car drives, and is set in race setup. AI cars get
Arcade's grip when you pick it. Online races always use Arcade.

- **Arcade**: the default. Big grip, quick steering, and forgiving grass. Hold
  the handbrake at speed to drift, and stay on the throttle to keep it going.
- **Sport**: realistic grip, with driver aids as a safety net.
- **Sim**: no aids at all. The car will spin if you ask it to.

**Nitro** fills on its own, and three times faster while the car is sliding. The
gauge turns yellow while a slide is filling it. The tank can't fill while the
nitro is burning.

**Engine sound**: a car's spec sets how it sounds and nothing else, and every car
keeps its nitro. `cylinders` runs from a clean, thin four up to a rough, heavy
sixteen, and 0 means electric. `turbos` is 0, 1, 2 or 4: one is a thin high
whistle that spools fast, and more are lower, louder and slower to build, with a
deeper blow-off. `supercharged` adds a whine that follows the revs. `raceExhaust`
is a race engine's smooth, high wail whatever its cylinder count, which is how
OPEN WHEELER's V6 differs from ROCKET's.

**Slipstream**: sit close behind another car at speed and its wake cuts your
drag, and on Arcade it pulls you along too. A meter appears above the dash. The
effect lasts about a second after you pull out, so time it to slingshot past.

**Midnight**: the darkest TIME. The circuit has no floodlights, so you see by your
own headlights, which end well short of what the camera shows, and by the moon.
Only the start line is lit. Amber and white studs mark the two edges of the road
beyond your beams, and a rival shows up by its lights. Cars start on high beam: **F**
is low beam and **G** switches high beam off, if you want to drive blind.

**Radio**: freestyle plays your own music, shuffled and non-stop, and nothing else
does. Drop MP3, OGG, FLAC or WAV files on the game window, or put them in the
`music` folder of LÖVE's save folder. MUSIC FOLDER in freestyle setup opens it, and
RADIO there sets the volume or turns it off. Song names come from the filenames.
The folder is `~/.local/share/love/spppd/music` on Linux and
`%APPDATA%\spppd\music` for the Windows build. Phones don't have the radio,
because their save folder can't be reached.

**Zoom** in Settings moves the camera between Near, Mid and Far.

The arrow at the top of the screen is a pace note: the direction of the next
corner, how tight it is (1 is a hairpin, 4 a fast kink) and when to brake. Turn
it off in Settings if you would rather learn the track.

## Track editor

Open it from the main menu. A track is one line of nodes through the middle of
the road, each with its own width on either side.

- Click to add a node, drag to move it, right click to delete it.
- Select a node to get its green width handles. Hold Shift to change both sides.
- Press **T** to drive the track at any time. Leaving the test drive brings you
  back with your work intact.
- Kerbs, gravel traps, barriers and the racing line are all worked out for you.
- The bottom left of the screen warns you if the layout has a problem.

Built-in tracks can be opened too. Saving one makes your own copy. Your tracks
are saved in LÖVE's save folder, and show up in race setup.

## Online

Two players can race through the relay in `server/`.

    make server
    ./dist/relay -addr :8080

Both players set SERVER to that machine's address under ONLINE and pick a car.
One picks HOST A ROOM, chooses the track, laps and time of day, and gets a room
code to read out. The other enters it and joins. UDP port 8080 has to be
reachable, and both players need the host's track installed.

The relay can also show a dashboard with its rooms, races, traffic and recent
activity, where you can close a room or ban an IP. Bans last until the relay
restarts.

    ./dist/relay -addr :8080 -dashboard 127.0.0.1:9090

Then open <http://localhost:9090>. The dashboard has no login, so keep it on
loopback.

## Tests

    make test          # simulation, rules, editor, settings, audio. No display needed.
    make test-visual   # plays the real game through its input handlers. Needs a display.
    make test-server   # relay server, with the race detector
    make test-online   # two clients through a real relay
    love . --perf      # frame timings for a race
    make apk-perf      # the same on a phone: read them with adb logcat | grep PERF

## How it fits together

- `src/sim/` is the game itself, with no drawing: `dynamics` (tyres, engine,
  assists), `track` (geometry, racing line, barriers), `race` (rules).
- `src/entities/` ties the car model to Box2D, which only handles collisions.
- `src/render/` draws the world: road shaders, car sprites, lighting, glow.
- `src/ai_controller.lua` drives through the same inputs as a player, so the AI
  obeys the same physics.
- `src/touch.lua` is the on-screen stick and buttons, layered over `src/input.lua`.
- `src/rumble.lua` turns what the car is doing into pad and phone vibration.

The simulation steps at a fixed 120 Hz, so the car behaves the same at any frame
rate. World units are pixels at 12 px per metre; the vehicle model is in SI units.
